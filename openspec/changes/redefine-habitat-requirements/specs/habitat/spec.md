# Habitat specification draft

## Purpose

Provide and adapt the colony's whole physical plant so its occupants and useful
activities have suitable accommodation, space, equipment, access and services.
Balance immediate provision, continued operation, resilience and improvement
against other colony needs.

This draft formalizes the maintainer's habitat exploration on 2026-10-25–26,
as traced in the [decision record](../../proposal.md#decision-provenance-and-review-boundary).
The scenarios are proposed acceptance examples, not separately approved tactics
or evidence of passing behavior. This active change does not replace the standing
[habitat requirements and evidence](../../../../../docs/requirements/habitat.md).
That page retains H1–H7 with their original inspection, execution and proof limits.

RG-HABITAT-001–003 preserve the existing accommodation, essential-service and useful
development identities. RG-HABITAT-004–011 make the agreed scope and tradeoffs
explicit. No implementation-completeness claim follows from this draft.

## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-001 — Provide suitable sleeping and resting accommodation

The governor SHALL provide suitable, usable sleeping and resting accommodation
for colony residents, hosted people and kept animals, accounting for their actual
needs and access. It SHALL compare shared, private and temporary arrangements by
their costs and consequences, reassessing changes in occupants and lost capacity.

#### Scenario: A barracks is preferable to individual bedrooms

- **WHEN** shared and private accommodation can meet the occupants' functional needs
- **THEN** the governor compares materials, construction, space, travel, thermal conditions, decoration, privacy and disturbed sleep
- **AND** a worthwhile barracks can remain the permanent arrangement without a required bedroom upgrade

#### Scenario: Guests or prisoners need accommodation

- **WHEN** hosted guests or held prisoners lack suitable sleeping places
- **THEN** their accommodation demand enters planning, including new provision when justified
- **AND** absence of a vacant bed does not erase their unmet need
- **AND** prisoner accommodation includes applicable containment and access requirements without assuming recruitment value

#### Scenario: Animals require different resting arrangements

- **WHEN** kept animals differ in shelter, temperature, resting or containment needs
- **THEN** their accommodation reflects those needs and the actual environment
- **AND** the governor does not impose a barn or manufactured bed on every animal

#### Scenario: Growth or damage leaves an immediate shortage

- **WHEN** an arrival or lost shelter leaves an occupant without suitable accommodation
- **THEN** the governor compares timely temporary provision with permanent alternatives
- **AND** unmet functional conditions remain visible while construction proceeds

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-002 — Keep essential facilities usable

The governor SHALL arrange the power, fuel, temperature control and upkeep needed
for useful facilities and occupied spaces. It SHALL distinguish causes of service
failure and compare work that addresses them. Affected service SHALL remain unmet
until observations establish usable operation; missing required facts SHALL not
establish recovery.

#### Scenario: Installed power capacity is sufficient but its generator is empty

- **WHEN** an empty generator can serve demand after receiving available fuel
- **THEN** the governor considers refuelling rather than treating the failure as proof of insufficient generating capacity

#### Scenario: A service remains unavailable after an order

- **WHEN** repair, refuelling, connection or temperature work has been ordered
- **THEN** the affected service remains deficient until its required operation is observed
- **AND** a receipt or an unread service does not establish restoration

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-003 — Develop facilities for the benefits they provide

The governor SHALL compare physical development with other useful colony work
through its expected consequences, including readiness, materials, shared labor,
operating costs and disruption. Benefits SHALL accrue only when usable provision
can produce them. Construction counts SHALL not substitute for useful outcomes,
and unread required facts SHALL prevent pricing the affected alternative.

#### Scenario: A facility cannot yet deliver its benefit

- **WHEN** a room or workstation needs unfinished construction, equipment, staffing or service before use
- **THEN** its comparison includes those prerequisites and their delay
- **AND** placing some of its components does not earn the completed facility's benefit

#### Scenario: Shared space and connectors support several functions

- **WHEN** functions could share a room or require corridors, doors or airlocks
- **THEN** the governor compares their actual effects, including access, travel, interference, disturbed sleep and thermal consequences
- **AND** shared prerequisites and benefits are accounted for without duplicate credit or spend
- **AND** a fixed room-compatibility table alone does not decide sharing

#### Scenario: No eligible user exists

- **WHEN** observed conditions establish that nobody can use a proposed facility during the assessed period
- **THEN** the facility receives no assumed benefit from those absent users
- **AND** known absence remains distinct from a missing required observation

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-004 — Provide suitable places for treatment and recovery

The governor SHALL compare care locations with medical needs, including existing
beds, temporary medical designation, improvised sites and equipped hospitals.
Comparison SHALL include treatment and recovery effects, readiness, movement,
exposure and displacement of other uses. A bed serving care SHALL not also count
as independently available sleeping or treatment capacity.

#### Scenario: Routine care can occur in a patient's own bed

- **WHEN** bedside care and transfer to a medical area are feasible alternatives
- **THEN** the governor compares their treatment, recovery, travel, movement and accommodation consequences
- **AND** temporary bed conversion includes its effect on sleeping availability and subsequent reassessment of bed use

#### Scenario: Surgery benefits from a better bed and room

- **WHEN** a cleaner or better-equipped location changes an operation's expected outcome
- **THEN** the governor compares that benefit with readiness, transfer and construction costs
- **AND** medical designation alone does not receive the benefits of equipment or conditions that are absent
- **AND** a separate hospital is not an unconditional prerequisite for every operation

#### Scenario: Field stabilization can avoid treatment delay

- **WHEN** a medical sleeping spot can enable useful treatment sooner than transport to an existing ward
- **THEN** the governor compares field care, transport and later transfer using their delay, exposure and treatment consequences
- **AND** placing and designating the spot does not establish that treatment occurred

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-005 — Compare complete facility and activity-site alternatives

The governor SHALL compare useful facility alternatives with feasible placement,
supporting infrastructure, timing and operating consequences included. It SHALL
consider reuse and temporary provision alongside construction where applicable.
Choosing equipment before its spatial consequences are assessed SHALL not exclude
a better complete alternative.

#### Scenario: A spot bridges workstation construction

- **WHEN** useful production can use an immediate spot or a constructed workstation
- **THEN** the governor compares the spot's actual performance and location costs with the workstation's construction, infrastructure, readiness and operating effects
- **AND** using a spot while constructing the workstation is an alternative
- **AND** neither permanent retention nor automatic upgrade is imposed regardless of value

#### Scenario: A spot designates an activity rather than an equipment substitute

- **WHEN** a ritual, marriage, party or similar activity needs a designated location
- **THEN** feasible locations are compared with the activity's access, capacity, surroundings and competing uses
- **AND** the location is not assigned a workstation-upgrade lifecycle merely because it is called a spot

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-006 — Provide spatial support for agriculture and animals

The governor SHALL develop fields, growing zones, pens and associated access,
shelter and services to support selected crop and animal alternatives. Spatial
capacity and consequences SHALL inform those alternatives, including usable land,
environmental conditions, containment, feeding access and shared work.

#### Scenario: Crop alternatives compete for useful land

- **WHEN** food and non-food crop alternatives need overlapping land or infrastructure
- **THEN** comparison includes their feasible sites, environmental suitability, worker access and supporting costs
- **AND** a nominal zone area does not establish useful growing capacity

#### Scenario: A pen exists without usable feeding access

- **WHEN** a proposed animal arrangement contains animals but does not provide the feeding access or shelter it requires
- **THEN** the missing provision remains part of the complete alternative and its unmet needs
- **AND** placing a fence or pen marker alone does not establish suitable animal accommodation

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-007 — Improve and prepare capacity when worthwhile

The governor SHALL continue considering physical improvements and spare capacity
against other useful work. Preparation SHALL account for foreseeable demand,
shortage consequences, lead time and temporary alternatives. No prescribed
finished base, room catalogue, upgrade sequence or fixed spare-capacity quota
SHALL determine the choice independently of those consequences.

#### Scenario: Better accommodation competes with another useful investment

- **WHEN** decoration, privacy, comfort or efficiency can improve an already functional facility
- **THEN** the governor weighs the improvement against its full cost and competing colony work
- **AND** functional adequacy neither mandates the upgrade nor permanently excludes worthwhile improvement

#### Scenario: Foreseeable arrivals or injuries could exceed capacity

- **WHEN** future occupancy, medical demand, seasonal changes or production demand could create a shortage
- **THEN** the governor compares early spare capacity with the likelihood and consequences of shortage and the speed of temporary provision
- **AND** a fixed number of empty beds or spare workstations does not determine the result

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-008 — Invest in resilience according to service consequences

The governor SHALL compare generation, storage, fuel reserves, insulation,
temperature control and passive alternatives by the consequences of interrupted
service, their full costs and shared vulnerabilities. Uniform uptime or a single
temperature target for every device and room SHALL not replace that assessment.

#### Scenario: Interrupted services have different consequences

- **WHEN** constrained resilience resources could protect refrigeration, treatment, sleeping conditions or workshop operation
- **THEN** their comparison includes each interruption's actual expected harm, duration and alternatives
- **AND** merely keeping more consumers powered does not establish the better choice

#### Scenario: A reserve shares the primary system's vulnerability

- **WHEN** nominal spare equipment depends on the same exposed fuel, distribution or environmental conditions as the primary service
- **THEN** the governor accounts for the shared failure exposure
- **AND** compares passive provision and other feasible alternatives without assuming nominal spare capacity guarantees service

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-009 — Develop useful defensive infrastructure

The governor SHALL compare feasible defensive arrangements using their protective
effects against assessed threats, construction, staffing, ammunition, power,
civilian exposure and interference with ordinary access. No mandatory killbox,
perimeter style or particular defensive structure SHALL define adequacy.

#### Scenario: Defensive arrangements impose different operating burdens

- **WHEN** walls, cover, firing positions, turrets or other supported arrangements offer different protective effects
- **THEN** comparison includes their threat-specific effects and full construction and operating costs
- **AND** an arrangement does not receive protection credit merely because its structures were placed

#### Scenario: Temporary defenses or retreat positions are useful

- **WHEN** a threat can be addressed by temporary cover or retreat geometry before permanent works are ready
- **THEN** those feasible arrangements are considered with their timing, access and civilian-exposure consequences
- **AND** protection policy supplies the threat and tactical effects rather than a prescribed base shape

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-010 — Maintain and redevelop with transition costs included

The governor SHALL compare repair, replacement, relocation, repurposing, retention,
storage and removal where feasible. It SHALL include construction, hauling,
resource recovery, changed travel and service disruption, arranging transition
provision as needed. Neither damage nor idleness SHALL automatically require
restoring or demolishing the original facility.

#### Scenario: A damaged generator no longer justifies restoration

- **WHEN** repair, another power source or retirement can address the colony's current service demand
- **THEN** the governor compares those alternatives rather than automatically rebuilding the original generator

#### Scenario: Redevelopment displaces occupied accommodation

- **WHEN** a worthwhile layout change would remove beds or interrupt other needed service
- **THEN** comparison includes the transition's service loss and feasible bridging arrangements
- **AND** replacement or temporary accommodation is made usable before removal where needed to maintain accommodation

#### Scenario: A spot or room outlives its original purpose

- **WHEN** a workstation replaces a spot, occupants leave or another demand ends
- **THEN** the governor reassesses retaining, repurposing, storing or removing the affected provision
- **AND** both retained capacity and removal costs enter the choice instead of assuming every idle facility must disappear

### Requirement: RG-HABITAT-011 — Verify functional provision and retain unmet needs

The governor SHALL distinguish functional requirements from costed preferences
and assess provision through observed usable service. It SHALL retain unmet needs
and material blockers during temporary or incomplete provision. Missing required
facts, nominal capacity and accepted placement orders SHALL not establish access,
functional containment, completed construction or successful use.

#### Scenario: Attractive accommodation is inaccessible or unusable

- **WHEN** decoration or nominal capacity is adequate but an intended occupant cannot use the bed or reach required facilities
- **THEN** the affected functional need remains unmet
- **AND** comfort or efficiency credit does not substitute for the missing function

#### Scenario: A staged facility has not achieved its intended outcome

- **WHEN** a medical spot, workbench, room or other facility has been placed but its required service is not observed
- **THEN** placement is tracked separately from usable provision and from treatment, production or other downstream outcomes
- **AND** the responsible unmet needs and blockers remain visible under fresh assessment
